# FREEDOM — 3 lb NHRL Combat Robot

**Villanova Club Robotics · January 2025 – May 2026**  
**Ethan Prescott — Team Lead & Driver**

FREEDOM is a 3 lb vertical-spinner combat robot built by a nine-member team for Villanova’s first appearance at the National Havoc Robot League (NHRL). This repository documents my contributions, design decisions, and lessons from the build.

## My contributions

- **Layout and CAD:** Researched existing 3 lb robots, established initial dimensions and weight targets, and modeled components in SolidWorks. Teammates owned the detailed armor and weapon designs.
- **Transmission design:** Sized a belt-and-pulley system so the weapon motor could be positioned inside the robot for protection.
- **Team coordination:** Connected subsystem work, component selection, fabrication, and the weight budget. Worked with the club president, Bradley, who guided part choices and handled university ordering.
- **Assembly and fabrication:** Assembled the robot after teammates completed controls work and soldering, cut the dead shaft using a bandsaw, and coordinated armor and weapon fabrication with an experienced CNC and laser-cutter operator.
- **Competition preparation:** Arranged backup armor and electronics, spent more than five hours testing and practicing driving, and coordinated last-minute pit repairs.

## Key design decision: protecting the weapon motor

My earlier class robot, Bread, used a motor connected directly to its weapon, leaving the motor exposed. For FREEDOM, I placed the motor inside the chassis and sized a belt-and-pulley transmission to drive the weapon.

This added packaging and transmission-design work, but gave the motor a more protected location.

## Constraints and competition results

The main constraint was the 3 lb weight limit. My initial planning budget allocated approximately 1 lb each to electronics, armor, and the weapon.

- **Inspection:** The robot weighed exactly 3 lb before an additional safety-switch holder was fitted. We removed armor material to return within the weight limit.
- **First match:** A radio connection that had worked during inspection failed before the match, resulting in disqualification.
- **Second match:** I coordinated the team’s adjustments to restore the radio connection and meet weight in time to compete. The rushed changes weakened the structure and armor, and we lost the match.

## Lessons for the next build

- Include inspection hardware in the original layout and weight budget.
- Reserve weight for integration changes.
- Finish the complete assembly earlier to allow more testing.
- Verify radio reliability and structural integrity before matches.
- Give subsystem owners clear next steps as well as deadlines.
- Consider the XP bracket for a first-time team.

These lessons now inform how I help guide newer teams as co-president of Villanova Club Robotics.
